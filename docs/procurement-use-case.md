# Procurement buyer scenario and evaluation targets

## Scenario

A procurement operator needs a short supplier agreement for Example Components with
Net 30 payment terms. They know the commercial intent but should not be expected to
manually compare every generated byte, recipient, signature field, policy finding, and
delivery option before release.

SignLatch structures the request, renders a reviewable PDF through the bounded Foxit
preparation adapter, records policy findings, and presents one exact review snapshot.
A distinct approver must acknowledge that snapshot. Any material mutation invalidates
the approval, and only an isolated dispatcher may consume a current approval once.

## Buyer value hypothesis

The product is intended to reduce review coordination without weakening authority. The
following are evaluation targets for a sandbox study, not measured customer results:

| Measure | Target | Evidence method |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Time from complete intent to review-ready fixture | under 2 minutes | timestamp workflow creation and review readiness |
| First-time reviewer completion | at least 4 of 5 reviewers without assistance | moderated task with fresh reviewers |
| Material mutation detection | 5 of 5 bound categories blocked | approval attack harness and visible browser journey |
| Duplicate external effects | zero duplicates across 20 concurrent requests | PostgreSQL integration race test |
| Unapproved provider dispatches | zero | dispatch denial audit events and provider correlation |
| Judge reproduction time | under 10 minutes | clean-checkout browser journey |

## What the current evidence proves

Fixture tests prove exact binding, denial after five mutation classes, restoration
denial, tenant isolation, durable one-unit budgeting, and a reproducible judge journey.
One real Foxit PDF preparation was captured separately as reversible provider evidence.
No customer time saving, legal outcome, live eSign delivery, or completed signer journey
is claimed yet.

## Sandbox evaluation protocol

1. Recruit five consenting reviewers who have not seen the interface.
2. Give each the same sanitized Example Components intent and no product walkthrough.
3. Record readiness time, completion time, assistance, and any misunderstood boundary.
4. Run one mutation per bound category and record whether the reviewer detects the
   invalidation before attempting release.
5. Store only aggregate timings and sanitized observations publicly; keep identities,
   document bytes, provider identifiers, and raw event payloads private.

Running the live dispatch or signer-completion portion still requires the independent
release decisions in [release-checklist.md](release-checklist.md).
